# What Are Large Language Models (LLMs)? | IBM

## Summary

The text explains large language models (LLMs) as deep learning models trained on vast data, using transformer architectures to handle natural language tasks. LLMs are statistical prediction machines that adapt to various applications, represent a major advancement in AI, and are widely used in both public and enterprise settings. They are trained using self-supervised learning, tokenization, and transformer networks with self-attention mechanisms. LLMs can be fine-tuned for specific tasks, including supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), and instruction tuning. Once trained, LLMs generate text by predicting tokens based on learned patterns, and they can be controlled through parameters like temperature and context windows. The text explains Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) as a method to connect pretrained models with external knowledge bases for more accurate responses. It discusses the complexity of building large language models (LLMs) and their accessibility via APIs. LLMs are used in various applications like text generation, summarization, code generation, and more. They also have limitations such as hallucinations, bias, and high resource demands. Mitigation strategies include AI governance and benchmarking. The history of LLMs includes early rule-based systems, neural networks, and the transformer architecture introduced in 2017. Modern LLMs like BERT, GPT, and T5 are highlighted, along with newer architectures like Mamba and Diffusion LLMs. The text also promotes IBM's AI solutions for business applications.
